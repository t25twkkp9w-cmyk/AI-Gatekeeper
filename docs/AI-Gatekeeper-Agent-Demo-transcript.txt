00:22  The business problem
Imagine a healthcare team proposing a staff policy assistant, while another team wants AI to draft patient messages. Both need a review, but their data, risks, and evidence requirements differ. Today, those reviews often depend on scattered documents, repeated questions, and unclear ownership. AI Gatekeeper Agent organizes that work. The intended impact is less reviewer preparation, more complete submissions, and decisions that can be reconstructed. Time savings are a hypothesis to measure, not a claimed result.

00:52  One enterprise workflow
The enterprise workflow has five stages: intake, evidence, risk and controls, human review, and decision. The requester supplies purpose, owner, tool, and data classification. The system checks the required evidence and tool eligibility, then prepares findings and controls. Missing evidence pauses the case. Requested changes send it back for reassessment. A recorded approval is a governance outcome, not automatic deployment.

01:16  Two use cases to follow
We will follow two examples. The staff policy assistant handles internal information and needs documented oversight within an approved scope. Patient message drafting introduces protected health information, external processing, and patient-facing outputs. That requires privacy evidence, evaluation, monitoring, and additional reviewers. The workflow adapts to the declared context instead of treating every request alike.

01:40  Technology serves the process
The experience layer is Next.js and React. FastAPI validates actions and protects case revisions. LangGraph coordinates conditional routes, state, checkpoints, and human interrupts. LangChain implements three specialists for intake, risk, and review preparation. OpenAI supplies advisory reasoning, with GPT four point one mini as the default model. SQLite stores cases, audit events, and checkpoints. A versioned JSON catalog supplies synthetic knowledge. LangSmith is the required observability layer.

02:09  Inside the model and tool loop
This is the agent at the center of the solution. Inputs include purpose, owner, data class, tool, evidence, and version. LangGraph coordinates the case, while LangChain specialists use the OpenAI model to select tools and synthesize findings. Read tools inspect evidence, retrieve policy, and look up approved tools. Knowledge returns as observations to the model. Outputs are risk findings, cited controls, a review packet, and the next action. The harness enforces rules and checkpoints. LangSmith traces execution. Humans retain decision authority.

02:40  The execution harness
The harness makes this more than a prompt. It validates inputs, maintains case revisions, persists checkpoints, and routes the next action. A register failure gets two total attempts before an explicit recovery pause. Model calls have a twenty-second timeout and one retry, while agent iterations are bounded. Model failures produce a warning and deterministic fallback. These controls make interruption and recovery visible rather than silently treating every call as successful.

03:07  Observe execution and decisions
Observability follows the same workflow. LangSmith connects orchestration to model and tool spans, with inputs, outputs, errors, latency, and token usage where available. Application monitoring surfaces workflow events and recoveries. Audit history answers a different question: who recorded what decision, with which rationale and evidence. Neither tracing nor a model explanation proves compliance. Now we will connect this architecture to the application.

07:35  Scale to production
Production scale-up has three workstreams. First, replace shared login with SSO and server-bound roles; protect data, secrets, and evidence provenance; use durable managed storage and tested recovery. Second, introduce queued workers, idempotency, concurrency controls, rate limits, cost budgets, and tested failure recovery. Evaluate policy grounding, tool selection, and decision-gate behavior before releases. Third, establish accountable owners, independent signoffs, audit retention, and incident processes. The current Render Free service and temporary storage are demonstration infrastructure.
